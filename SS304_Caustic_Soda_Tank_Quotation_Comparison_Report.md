## SECTION 5 — NaOH SERVICE REQUIREMENTS & CHEMICAL RISKS

This section covers critical process and chemical engineering requirements specific to Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH) storage. **None of these risks are addressed by any vendor.**

### 5.1 NaOH Properties Relevant to Tank Design

| Property | Value | Design Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Chemical name | Sodium Hydroxide (Caustic Soda) | Class 8 Corrosive — SCDF/NEA regulated |
| Typical storage concentration | 25–50% w/w | Verify with process team |
| Specific gravity (25% NaOH) | ~1.27 | Foundation load: 1,600 × 1.27 = 2,032 t |
| Specific gravity (48% NaOH) | ~1.50 | Foundation load: 1,600 × 1.50 = 2,400 t |
| Specific gravity (100% NaOH) | 2.13 | Not stored as pure solid in these tanks |
| Freezing point (25% NaOH) | ~−25°C | Not an issue in Singapore |
| Freezing point (50% NaOH) | +12°C | May solidify in cool conditions |
| Flash point | None (non-flammable) | No ATEX requirement for tank |
| UN classification | UN 1824 (solution) | Placarding and containment required |

> **Action required:** Client must confirm NaOH concentration before tank design is finalised. At >48% concentration, the fluid specific gravity increases the foundation loads and may change the material compatibility assessment.

### 5.2 Caustic Stress Corrosion Cracking (SCC) — CRITICAL RISK

**This is the single most important material risk for this project. No vendor has addressed it.**

Austenitic stainless steels (SS304, SS304L) are susceptible to Caustic Stress Corrosion Cracking (SCC), also called caustic embrittlement, when exposed to NaOH above certain temperature/concentration thresholds.

| NaOH Concentration | Safe Temperature (SS304L) | Risk Level |
|---|---|---|
| < 25% | Up to 60°C | LOW — generally safe |
| 25–50% | Up to 40°C | MODERATE — monitor temperature |
| 25–50% | 40–60°C | HIGH — SCC risk exists |
| > 50% | Any elevated temp | VERY HIGH — SCC highly likely |
| Any | > 80°C | CRITICAL — SS304L unsuitable |

**Root cause:** High residual weld stresses + NaOH environment + elevated temperature = intergranular cracking. The combination of NaOH penetrating grain boundaries and tensile residual stresses from welding drives crack propagation, often with no visible external corrosion.

**Mitigation options:**
1. **Confirm NaOH temperature will remain <40°C** — the most important action item
2. If temperature may exceed 40°C: require **full PWHT** (stress relief at 900°C for 30 min/25mm) — note this conflicts with 304L grade selection (PWHT of 304L can cause sensitisation if done incorrectly)
3. Alternatively, upgrade material to **SS316L** or **nickel alloy (Alloy 400/Alloy 200)** for higher temperature/concentration service
4. **Low delta ferrite weld metal** — reducing residual stresses in weld heat-affected zones
5. **Shot peening** of weld zones to convert tensile to compressive residual stresses

> 🔴 **CRITICAL RISK:** If NaOH is stored above 40°C at concentrations >25%, or above 60°C at any concentration, SS304L is NOT a suitable material. This must be confirmed by the client's process team and a materials engineer before PO award. No vendor has raised this risk.

### 5.3 Sensitisation Risk in SS304 (Credo's Grade)

- Standard SS304 has max carbon of 0.08% vs SS304L's max of 0.030%
- During welding, the heat-affected zone (HAZ) of SS304 can reach 425–870°C
- At this temperature range, chromium carbide precipitates at grain boundaries — this is sensitisation
- Sensitised SS304 in NaOH service is vulnerable to intergranular corrosion and SCC
- **SS304L** (low carbon) resists sensitisation because there is insufficient carbon to form chromium carbides
- **Credo must confirm SS304L is used, not standard SS304**

### 5.4 Iron Contamination Risk

- During fabrication, if carbon steel tools, grinding discs, or wire brushes are used on the stainless steel:
  - Free iron is embedded in the SS surface
  - In the presence of NaOH, this iron causes localised pitting
- **Requirement:** All fabrication must use SS-dedicated tools only (no carbon steel)
- **Post-fabrication:** Full passivation per ASTM A380 (not just HF acid cleaning) removes iron contamination and restores passive film
- This must be specified in the ITP and welding procedure

### 6.2 Critical API 650 Non-Conformances

**NCR-01 — Shell Weld NDT Method (FES — CRITICAL)**
API 650 §8.1 requires 100% RT or UT of all vertical shell butt joints. For tanks over 36m diameter with shell thickness <13mm, or tanks designed to the one-foot method, RT is mandatory for all vertical seams. PT is a surface-only examination (penetrates 0–3mm) and **cannot detect subsurface defects** such as porosity, incomplete fusion, slag inclusions, or cracks in the weld root. PT as the sole NDT method is a direct violation of API 650 §8.1 regardless of the ITP.

**NCR-02 — No Vacuum Box Test (All vendors)**
API 650 §8.6 mandates vacuum box testing of all horizontal and vertical bottom plate welds at minimum 6 psi vacuum. This detects bottom plate weld defects before hydrotest. Not one vendor mentions this mandatory test.

**NCR-03 — No WPS/PQR (All vendors)**
API 650 §7.1 requires all welding to be per qualified procedures (WPS) supported by Procedure Qualification Records (PQR) per ASME Section IX. This is non-negotiable. No vendor references these documents in their quotation.

**NCR-04 — No Mill Certificates (All vendors)**
API 650 §5.2 requires all steel to be furnished with certified mill test reports (MTRs) showing chemical composition and mechanical properties. For ASTM A240-304L, carbon content ≤0.030% must be verified per heat by MTR. Fabrication cannot proceed without this.

**NCR-05 — Freeboard/Capacity Shortfall (FES)**
FES tank at 11,450mm × 15,400mm provides only 1,585 m³ geometric volume. With API 650-required freeboard, working capacity falls to 1,506–1,554 m³. The tank must be redesigned with a minimum shell height of 15,840 mm to achieve 1,600 m³ working capacity.

**NCR-06 — Wind Girder Omission (Credo and Synergex)**
API 650 §5.9 requires a top wind girder for tanks where the H/D ratio exceeds certain limits. Both Credo (H/D=2.05) and Synergex (H/D=2.30) require a top wind girder and likely an intermediate shell stiffener ring to prevent shell buckling under wind load or vacuum conditions.

**NCR-07 — Material Grade Ambiguity (Credo)**
Credo specifies "SS304" without confirming the L-grade. For welded construction per API 650, the material must meet ASTM A240 Grade 304L (max C=0.030%). Standard 304 (max C=0.080%) is susceptible to sensitisation in the weld HAZ and is less suitable for NaOH service.

**NCR-08 — Delta Ferrite Content (All vendors)**
API 650 and ASME Section IX require austenitic SS weld metal to contain 3–8% ferrite (FN 3–8 per WRC-1992 diagram) to prevent hot cracking. No vendor mentions ferrite content control in their NDT/weld quality plan.
